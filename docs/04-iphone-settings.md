# 04 — iPhone camera settings

Written for iPhone Pro / Pro Max models with 48MP. Menu names move a little between iOS versions,
so if something isn't exactly where described, search for it in the Settings app (swipe down on the Settings screen).

## Set these once

Settings → Camera:

| Setting | Set to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Formats → Camera Capture** | High Efficiency | Saves photos as HEIC: half the size of JPG, same quality. |
| **Formats → Photo Mode** | 24MP (daily) | Good balance of detail and size. You switch to 48MP when it matters (below). |
| **Formats → Resolution Control** | On | Puts a quick switch for 12/24/48MP in the camera. |
| **Formats → Apple ProRAW** | On | Lets you turn RAW on/off in the camera when you want it. |
| **Formats → Pro Default** | HEIF Max | What the camera uses when you tap the format button. HEIF Max = 48MP HEIC. |
| **Grid** | On | Shows the rule-of-thirds lines. The single most useful composition aid. |
| **Level** | On | Shows when the phone is perfectly straight. Crooked horizons are the #1 beginner mistake. |
| **Preserve Settings** | Turn on Creative Controls and Exposure Adjustment | Camera remembers your choices instead of resetting. |
| **Lens Correction** | On | Fixes stretched faces at the edges of ultra-wide shots. |
| **Prioritize Faster Shooting** | On | Takes more shots when you tap fast. Turn off only if you want max quality per shot. |

Also: Settings → Apps → Photos → *Transfer to Mac or PC* → **Keep Originals**. Otherwise photos get converted to JPG when you copy them to your PC.

## HEIF vs ProRAW — which one when

| Format | Size per photo (48MP, approx.) | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **HEIF 24MP** (default) | 2–5 MB | Everyday, people, moving things, lots of shots. |
| **HEIF Max 48MP** | 5–10 MB | Landscapes, architecture, anything you might crop or print. |
| **ProRAW Max 48MP** | 50–100 MB | Hard light (bright sky + dark ground), sunsets, photos you'll edit seriously. |

- **HEIC** is already "edited" by the iPhone. Good as-is, limited room to change.
- **ProRAW** keeps much more data. You can recover blown skies and dark shadows far better. But it fills your storage fast, so use it on purpose, not for everything.
- 48MP works best in good light on the main (1x) camera. In low light, Night mode or with flash, the iPhone may save at a lower resolution. That is normal.
- To check what each lens supports on your model, tap the format button (top of the camera screen) and switch lenses: it shows the resolution.

## Inside the Camera app

**Focus and exposure**
- **Tap** on your subject: the iPhone focuses and sets brightness for that spot.
- After tapping, **slide your finger up or down** next to the yellow box (the sun icon): brighter / darker.
- **Tap and hold** on the subject: "AE/AF LOCK". Focus and brightness stay locked while you recompose. Great for sunsets and for people in front of bright backgrounds.

**Exposure compensation (the ± control)**
Open the camera controls (tap the arrow at the top, or swipe up on the viewfinder; on recent models, light double-press the Camera Control button and choose Exposure).
- **−0.3 to −0.7** for sunsets, bright beaches and white buildings. It protects the sky. Shadows can be lifted later; pure white can't be fixed.
- **+0.3** for backlit people if you aren't using flash.
Rule: **when in doubt, a bit darker is safer than a bit brighter.**

**Lenses**
- Use the lens buttons (0.5x, 1x, 2x, and the telephoto). These are real lenses or high-quality crops.
- Avoid pinching to zoom in between or beyond them. That's digital zoom and loses detail. Better: shoot at the nearest lens and crop later (48MP gives you room).

**Modes worth knowing**
- **Portrait**: blurred background for people, pets, food. Keep 2–2.5 m away.
- **Night mode**: turns on by itself in the dark. Hold still for the countdown, or brace the phone on something.
- **Burst**: hold the shutter and slide it left (or set volume-up to burst in Settings → Camera). For festivals, kids, sports.
- **Live Photo**: can be turned into a long exposure later (smooth waterfalls). Uses more storage.
- **Macro**: turns on when you get close. Small flowers, food details, textures.

**Photographic Styles**
They change the iPhone's built-in look (warmer, cooler, more contrast). On recent models they can be changed after shooting.
If you'll edit with this tool, leave the style on **Standard** so you start from a neutral photo.

**Flash**: keep it **off** for most shots. It flattens faces. Use it only for backlit people in daylight (fill flash).

## Quick checklist before you shoot

1. Wipe the lens (shirt corner). Fingerprints = foggy, glowing photos.
2. Is the horizon level? Watch the Level line.
3. Tap your subject to focus.
4. Sky too bright? Slide exposure down a bit.
5. Will you crop or edit seriously? Switch to HEIF Max or RAW Max first.
