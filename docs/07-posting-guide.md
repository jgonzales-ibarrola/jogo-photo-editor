# 07 — Posting guide

How we go from photos on your iPhone to a post people stop and like.
No algorithm secrets here: nobody outside Instagram/Facebook knows the exact rules.
These are the basics that consistently help, plus how to test what works for *your* followers.

## Our workflow

1. **You:** copy originals (Keep Originals) into a new folder, e.g. `photos\in\test3`.
2. **You:** tell Claude "edit test3 for posting". Add anything you know, e.g. "this is food" or "make it warm".
3. **Claude:** picks look + crop from [my-style.md](my-style.md), edits, checks each final photo, saves to `photos\out\test3\post`.
4. **Claude:** suggests which photos to post, the order, and a caption idea.
5. **You:** give feedback ("too blue", "love it"). Claude saves it in my-style.md and fixes what's needed.
6. **You:** copy the `post` folder to your phone/safe folder and post.
7. **You (after 2–3 days):** check the post's Insights and tell Claude what did well. We log it below.

## Choosing photos

- **Post fewer, better.** 3–5 strong photos beat 15 similar ones. Each photo should add something new.
- **Drop near-duplicates.** Same pose, same spot → keep the best one only (sharpest, best faces, eyes open).
- **Mix shot types**: one wide (place), one medium (people), one close (detail/emotion).

## Carousel order

| Slot | What goes here | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1st** | The strongest, most eye-catching photo. Often the group or the scene with the landmark. | It's the only one people see while scrolling. It decides if they stop. |
| **Middle** | Variety: couples, solo shots, details, candid moments. | Keeps people swiping. Swipes count as engagement. |
| **Last** | A strong closer: a fun, funny or emotional shot. | Leaves a good final impression; people often go back to the first. |

Example (test2, Cagsawa): **group (0084) → couple (0088) → solo (0098)**.
Big, colorful group opener with Mayon; then closer, more personal shots.

## Shape and quality

- Use **4:5** for every photo in a carousel. The first photo sets the shape for all of them.
- Our output (1638×2048, JPG) is already the right size. Don't re-edit or add filters in the app on top — it stacks and over-processes.
- Upload over Wi-Fi when you can. Some apps lower quality on weak mobile data.
- Instagram: turn on *Settings → Media quality → Upload at highest quality*.

## Caption

- **First line matters most** — it's all people see before "more". Make it short and specific.
- Say **where** and **what**: "Cagsawa Ruins with Mayon hiding in the clouds."
- **Ask a simple question** to get comments: "Have you been to Albay?" / "Which photo is your favorite, 1, 2 or 3?"
- **Tag the people** in the photos (with their OK). They and their friends see it.
- **Add the location tag** (e.g. Cagsawa Ruins). People browsing that place can find you.
  Your files have GPS removed; the location tag is a choice you make per post.
- **Hashtags**: 3–5 relevant ones, not 30. Place + topic, e.g. `#Albay #MayonVolcano #CagsawaRuins #PhilippinesTravel`.

## When to post

There's no universal best time. In general, people scroll most in the **early morning, lunch, and evening (around 7–10 PM)**,
and more on weekends. The real answer is in your own **Insights → Audience → Most active times** (Instagram professional/creator account)
or Facebook Page insights. Post then, and adjust after a few posts.

## After posting

- **Reply to comments in the first hour.** Conversations push the post to more people.
- **Share to your Story** with a sticker or "new post" — brings your followers to it.
- **Don't delete and re-post** a post that "did badly". Learn from it instead.
