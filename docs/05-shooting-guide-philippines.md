# 05 — Shooting guide (Philippines)

Good photos come from three things: **light, composition, timing**. Editing only polishes them.

---

## 1. Light in the Philippines

The Philippines is close to the equator. That changes how light behaves:

- **Midday sun (about 9 AM – 3 PM) is harsh.** The sun is almost straight overhead. Faces get dark eye sockets, colors wash out, skies blow out white.
  → Shoot people in open shade (under a roof edge, building shadow, tree). Shoot landscapes early or late.
- **Golden hour is short.** The sun goes down steeply near the equator, so the warm, soft light lasts roughly 20–40 minutes, not the hour you read about online.
  → Be in position 30 minutes before sunset. Don't arrive at sunset.
- **Sunrise and sunset are both around 5:30–6:30**, AM and PM, depending on the month. Check a weather app for the exact time.
- **Blue hour** (about 15–25 min after sunset): deep blue sky with city lights on. Best time for Manila skylines, Intramuros, lit churches.
- **Overcast and rainy-season days (about June–November) are good for people.** Clouds are a giant softbox: even light, no squinting.
- **Dry season (about December–May)**: clear skies, best for beaches and landscapes. March–May is hottest and hazy: shoot early morning.

### Practical problems here

- **Lens fogging**: going from an air-conditioned room or car to outside fogs the lens. Wait 5–10 minutes. Don't wipe repeatedly, it smears.
- **Heat**: in direct sun the iPhone can overheat, dim the screen and stop the flash. Keep it in shade between shots.
- **Sunscreen and sweat on the lens** make everything glow and blur. Wipe the lens before every important shot.
- **Rain**: shoot from under cover. Reflections on wet streets at night look great.

## 2. Composition — the ones that matter

Turn on **Grid** and **Level** first (see [04](04-iphone-settings.md)).

**Rule of thirds** — Put your subject on a grid line or where two lines cross, not dead center.
Horizon on the lower line = sky is the star. Upper line = land/sea is the star. Never through the middle, unless it's a reflection.
*Example: a bangka boat on the left third, open sea to the right.*

**Leading lines** — Roads, rails, rice terrace edges, bridges, shorelines pulling the eye into the photo.
*Example: the curving rows of the Banaue or Batad rice terraces; a road through Batanes hills.*

**Framing** — Shoot through something: a doorway, church arch, window, palm leaves. It adds depth and focus.
*Example: an Intramuros archway framing a calesa.*

**Symmetry** — Center the subject when the scene is symmetrical. Breaks the thirds rule on purpose.
*Example: the front of a Spanish-era church (Paoay, Miagao, Baclayon), a pier, a reflection in still water.*

**Foreground interest** — Put something close at the bottom of a landscape: rocks, shells, flowers, a boat rope. Get low.
*Example: coral rocks in front of an El Nido or Siargao sunset.*

**Fill the frame** — Get closer. Remove everything that isn't the subject.
*Example: a jeepney's chrome horse and painted side; a vendor's hands making taho; a plate of sisig.*

**Negative space** — Lots of empty space around a small subject. Calm and clean.
*Example: one person on a long white beach, mostly sky and sand.*

**Patterns and repetition** — Rows of the same thing; break the pattern with one different item for extra impact.
*Example: stacked fruit at a palengke, rows of parols at Christmas, salt beds, fish nets.*

**Layers** — Foreground, middle, background in one shot.
*Example: palm trees (front), a beach (middle), islands (back) in Coron.*

**Change your height** — Most people shoot at chest height. Get low for drama, go high for overview, shoot food from straight above or at 45°.

## 3. By subject

**Beaches**
- Shoot early morning: calm water, no crowds, soft light.
- Midday: put the sun behind you so the water turns turquoise. Use the `beach` preset.
- Slide exposure down a little: white sand fools the camera.

**Rice terraces, mountains, Batanes, Sagada**
- Mornings often have fog and clouds below you: shoot early.
- Use leading lines and a person for scale.
- HEIF Max or ProRAW Max. Preset: `landscape`.

**Streets — Manila, Binondo, Escolta, Cebu, provincial towns**
- Jeepneys, tricycles, sari-sari stores, signs: lots of color. Preset: `vivid` or `bw`.
- Find a good background and wait for someone interesting to walk into it.
- Burst mode for moving subjects.

**Festivals — Sinulog, Ati-Atihan, Dinagyang (January), Panagbenga (February), Pahiyas (May), MassKara (October)**
- Burst mode, 1x or 2x lens, get to the front early.
- Fill the frame with faces, costumes and hands, not whole crowds.
- Bright costumes in harsh sun: slide exposure down a bit.

**Food**
- Window light from the side, no flash, no overhead fluorescent light if you can avoid it.
- Top-down for flat dishes (kare-kare, pancit). 45° for tall food (halo-halo, burgers).
- Wipe the plate edges. Preset: `food`.

**People / portraits**
- Open shade or overcast. Never noon sun on a face.
- Focus on the eyes (tap them). Portrait mode at 2x.
- Put the light source beside or in front of them, not behind (unless you want a silhouette).

**Night / city lights (BGC, Makati, Manila Bay)**
- Blue hour beats full night.
- Brace the phone on a wall or railing. Let Night mode finish its countdown.
- Preset: `night`.

## 4. Respect and safety

- Ask before photographing people up close: **"Pwede po ba?"** A smile and asking first usually gets a yes and a better photo.
- Ask parents before photographing children.
- In churches: no flash, be quiet during mass, some areas may not allow photos.
- Some government buildings, military areas and airports don't allow photos. Respect signs and guards.
- Keep your phone secured in crowds (wrist strap). Don't hold it out at the curbside in busy streets.
- This tool removes GPS from edited photos by default. Keep it that way for photos of your home or family.

---

## 5. How to actually get better

1. **Shoot every day, one subject at a time.** One week only leading lines. Next week only shadows. Etc.
2. **Take 3 versions of every scene**: wide, medium, close. Pick the best later.
3. **Run `python -m jogo info` on your best and worst photos.** Look at what auto-fix had to repair. If it always says "too dark", adjust exposure in the camera.
4. **Copy photographers you like.** Find one photo, figure out the light, angle and composition, then recreate it.
5. **Edit less than you think.** If people notice the edit before the photo, it's too much.
